# Fit and feedback

## Fit criteria

`Fits original idea: yes` requires all of the following:

1. **Paragraph role** — the paragraph performs the role assigned by the
   outline rather than drifting into another section.
2. **Semantic coverage** — every required idea is present in substance; keyword
   overlap alone is not enough.
3. **Required detail** — material mechanisms, qualifications, examples, scope,
   and relationships from the detailed reference are not lost.
4. **Factual and citation accuracy** — claims, names, numbers, dates, technical
   terms, quotations, citation links, and attribution agree with the supplied
   reference or are clearly flagged as unresolved.
5. **No contradiction** — the paragraph does not reverse, overstate, or conflict
   with the reference or another accepted paragraph.
6. **Audience coherence** — a reader in the supplied audience can follow how
   the ideas connect, even if the prose is still stylistically imperfect.
7. **User wording** — all ordinary paragraph prose comes from the user's writing
   contributions and approved edits.

If any criterion fails, use `not yet`. Do not lower the gate merely because the
paragraph sounds fluent, and do not fail it merely because it differs in style
or vocabulary from the reference.

## Feedback contract

After every contribution, provide:

- **Covered:** name the ideas currently expressed, without praising or
  paraphrasing the paragraph into a replacement draft.
- **Missing or inaccurate:** identify the most material gap, incorrect claim,
  lost qualification, citation issue, contradiction, or misplaced idea. Use
  “Nothing material” only when the fit gate passes.
- **Writing feedback:** optionally give one observation about clarity,
  connection, redundancy, scope, grammar, or reader orientation. Describe the
  issue; do not supply substitute wording.
- **Next writing prompt:** ask exactly one question. When fit passes, ask whether
  the user accepts the paragraph or wants to revise it.

Keep feedback proportionate to the current stage. Early fragments can be
semantically useful without being grammatically complete. Prioritize missing
meaning before surface polish.

## Fidelity and distance from a reference

Evaluate ideas, not word swaps. If a user-authored paragraph follows a
third-party passage too closely in sequence, distinctive phrasing, or sentence
structure, do not call it safely “in their own words” merely because several
synonyms changed. Explain the closeness at a high level, preserve the working
paragraph unchanged, and ask the user to restate the underlying point from
their understanding. Required exact terms and properly marked quotations are
exceptions, not permission to reproduce surrounding prose.

Paraphrased third-party ideas still need whatever citation or attribution the
context requires. Preserve exact quotations exactly, retain their citation,
and keep them visibly distinct from the user's prose.
