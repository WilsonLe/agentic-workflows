# Concept-centric synthesis

Use source notes to populate the matrix, then synthesize across sources.

The matrix records:

- concept or debate;
- source identifier and evidence locator;
- definition or theoretical position;
- method and population/setting;
- finding or argument;
- disagreement or counterevidence;
- limitation or boundary condition;
- chronological role;
- gap or implication.

Do not use author names or paper titles as the primary organizing headings unless a justified
intellectual chronology is itself the question.

For each material synthesis claim:

1. Give it a stable `CLAIM-*` identifier.
2. Map supporting and contradicting `SRC-*` identifiers.
3. State whether it is a source-supported finding, reviewer interpretation, or proposal.
4. Record qualifications and boundary conditions.
5. Test rival explanations and preserve unresolved conflict.

A claim supported by several similar studies is not automatically generalizable. A disagreement
is not automatically resolved by source count. Explain how study design, concepts, settings,
populations, periods, and evidence quality affect the interpretation.
