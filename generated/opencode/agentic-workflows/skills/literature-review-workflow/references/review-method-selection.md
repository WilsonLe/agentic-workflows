# Review method selection

Choose the method from the intended contribution, not from a preferred label.

| Family | Use when the defensible contribution is | Required emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative | A contextual account of a broad or practice-facing question | Clear aims, transparent discovery description, balanced referencing, evidence levels, endpoints, and explicit coverage limits |
| Integrative | Critique and synthesis across diverse methods or traditions to produce a framework or new perspective | Conceptual integration, methodological heterogeneity, critique, and the derivation of the proposed framework |
| Critical | An argued reassessment of assumptions, methods, or dominant interpretations | Explicit evaluative standpoint, counterevidence, rival explanations, and disciplined distinction between evidence and judgment |
| Conceptual/theoretical | Clarification, comparison, extension, or integration of concepts and theories | Definitions, construct boundaries, propositions, mechanisms, and explanatory gaps |
| State-of-the-art | A time-bounded account of the newest direction, debate, or research frontier | Justified recency boundary, continuity with seminal work, emerging disagreements, and likely next questions |

Record the chosen family and why it fits the purpose, question, audience, source diversity, and
intended output. A review may combine emphases, but it must name a primary family and explain the
combination.

## Hard route boundary

Stop and route to the Systematic Literature Review plugin when the user requests or the product
requires any of the following:

- systematic, scoping, rapid, mapping, umbrella, or meta-analysis conduct;
- exhaustive or reproducible coverage claims;
- PRISMA reporting or a PRISMA flow diagram;
- protocol registration;
- formal risk-of-bias aggregation, quantitative pooling, or systematic screening.

Do not use “systematic” merely to mean careful. Do not retrofit an iterative or purposive search
into a systematic-review claim.
