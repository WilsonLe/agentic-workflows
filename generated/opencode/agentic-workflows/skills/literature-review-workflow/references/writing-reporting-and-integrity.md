# Writing, reporting, and integrity

Report enough method for a reader to understand what the review did and did not cover:

- purpose, questions, primary review family, and why it fits;
- boundaries, discovery surfaces, sampling approach, selection heuristics, and stopping rule;
- evidence verification and critical-reading approach;
- synthesis architecture;
- access, language, date, disciplinary, and method limitations;
- unresolved conflicts, thin areas, missing full texts, and likely omissions;
- tool or AI contributions and human verification at the required granularity.

Use accurate phrases such as “purposive and iterative discovery” or “a transparent narrative
review.” Do not claim systematic search, exhaustive coverage, reproducibility equivalent to a
systematic review, PRISMA compliance, or meta-analysis completion.

An outline or draft is optional. For assessed work, confirm institutional rules and route through
the academic-writing approval workflow. Unknown rules block drafting, not method planning.

Humanizer may improve clarity and voice after evidence checks. It must not alter quotations,
identifiers, findings, certainty, disclosure, or provenance, and must never be used to conceal AI
assistance.
