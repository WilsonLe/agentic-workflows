# Create, publish and improve a useful page

Read when producing or revising an article, guide, comparison or landing page.
For a utility, use this process for its supporting content and hand the software
requirements to the project's engineering workflow. Keep the asset's brief,
evidence, draft links, checks and release state in the existing cycle record or
linked project documents, using the [cycle template](../templates/growth-cycle.md).

These are working checkpoints, not separate approval rounds. Continue authorized
research and drafting without waiting at every outline or revision. Ask only for
unresolved material facts or decisions. Publishing, distribution and product changes
still need authority covering the actual destination and action.

## 1. Turn the selected query into a content brief

Use the shortlist from [search research](search-research.md), the product facts,
existing content inventory, audience and sprint capacity. State:

- primary query and related intents that one page can satisfy;
- reader, starting situation, constraints and the result they should achieve;
- intent, format, existing/new URL and reason to update or create;
- the question the opening must answer and the page's distinct contribution;
- the product's supported role, useful next action and measurable outcome;
- owner, deadline, effort cap, language and approved voice/style examples if available.

Choose format by job: a guide teaches a task; a comparison supports a decision
using named criteria; a landing page explains a specific use case and next step.
Do not make every query a generic blog post. Reuse a page already serving the intent.

**Output:** a brief that explains who the page helps, what it delivers, why it
deserves to exist and how the reader reaches the next useful product action.

## 2. Assemble evidence and demonstrations

Use observed search-result gaps as research questions, not an outline to copy.
Read relevant primary sources and the product's actual documentation or authorized
runtime. For material claims retain the source, location, capture date, scope and
what it supports. Verify current pricing, limits, compatibility and comparisons;
record when a claim is supplied but not independently checked.

Where useful, perform the task with permitted sample inputs. Keep steps, actual
outputs, limitations and screenshot references. An unavailable feature stays
unknown; never simulate a real product result or invent a testimonial, benchmark,
firsthand experience or customer story. Label illustrative examples as illustrative.
Use public or authorized sample data and visuals whose use is permitted; remove
private information from publication assets. Image generation can illustrate an
idea but cannot substitute for evidence of an interface or product result.

**Output:** a compact evidence packet mapping claims and demonstrations to sources,
plus missing proof, its owner and the affected passages. Draft around a gap or mark
it for resolution. An unsupported material claim cannot survive into publication.

## 3. Design the outline around the reader's task

Write a working title and direct opening answer. Order sections so the reader can
act: prerequisites/input → steps or decision criteria → example/result → relevant
limitations/troubleshooting → useful next action. Adapt this order to the format.
Map each substantive section to evidence, a demonstration or a clear explanation.
Include a FAQ only for real unresolved questions, not to repeat the article.

For comparisons, set consistent criteria and verify each alternative under the
same conditions. Explain when another option fits better. For a utility, specify
input/output, failure cases and the working result the engineering owner must
verify. A proposed tool is not evidence that a tool exists.

**Output:** an outline with each section's purpose, supporting material and CTA
placement. Resolve missing essentials before presenting the outline as ready to draft.

## 4. Write the draft and prepare supporting assets

Write the complete page, answering the central question early. Include specific
steps, examples, useful tables or calculations and limitations where they help
the reader. Cite claims near their support. Use natural query language; let task
coverage determine length rather than a keyword-density or word-count target.
Keep the product bridge connected to a step the product actually supports. State
relevant price, signup or input restrictions honestly before the user acts.

Use approved writing samples and product terminology for voice. When voice editing
is requested and available, use `humanizer`, preserving facts, meaning and sources.
Visuals need a purpose: show an input, a step, a result or a comparison. Produce
captions, useful alt text and asset/provenance references; use real captures for
interface instructions. Route specialized media work to an available appropriate
skill only when needed and authorized. Flag unresolved assets in the draft.

**Output:** a complete draft with title, opening, body, source links, CTA copy,
internal-link targets and required visual assets, plus explicit remaining gaps.

## 5. Review facts, usefulness and language

Check the draft in separate passes; one agent can perform them without delegation:

- **Evidence:** trace material claims to support; reproduce instructions where
  feasible; check calculations, dates and product/competitor details. Remove or
  resolve unsupported claims. Never imply a demonstration was run when it was not.
- **Reader outcome:** follow the page from its stated starting situation. Can the
  reader complete the job? Fix missing steps, misleading promises, irrelevant
  sections and a product CTA that does not match the intent.
- **Editorial:** match the approved voice, simplify vague prose, remove repetition
  and exaggerated claims, and check that titles, links, captions and limitations
  agree with the body. Editing cannot strengthen a claim beyond its evidence.

**Output:** a revised draft with material issues resolved or explicitly blocking
publication, plus a short record of what was checked and what remains unverified.
Do not create extra reviewer agents unless the user or calling workflow asks for them.

## 6. Prepare and inspect the actual page

Use the site's existing CMS/components and publishing process. Prepare the URL,
page title, description, canonical intent and descriptive internal links. Check
index eligibility for the intended release state; keep private previews private.
Structured data, if appropriate, must describe visible content and satisfy current
guidance. No special AI markup or ranking outcome follows from this workflow.

Inspect the rendered preview when available: headings/order, tables, images,
captions, alt text, narrow layout, keyboard use, readable text and working links.
Follow the CTA to its intended destination. Apply the [measurement contract](measurement.md)
and verify a permitted test event reaches its receiver where tracking is in scope.
Record a missing preview/runtime or analytics access as an unverified check, not a
pass. If the agreed release criteria require it, keep publication blocked.

**Output:** a publishable candidate with draft/version, preview URL, metadata,
assets, check results, blockers, publication owner and rollback/version reference.
An unresolved critical claim or broken essential task/CTA blocks release.

## 7. Publish and verify within the actual authorization

Present the candidate and any decision still needed. Reuse explicit authorization
for the exact site, page and action; a content-creation request alone leaves the
page draft. Use the site's release process and any applicable engineering gates.
Keep the previous version or recovery route for an update.

After authorized publication, read back the live URL, intended version, main
content, visuals, metadata, links and CTA. Recheck measurement receipt if in scope.
Record timestamp and outcome. If release fails or state is ambiguous, inspect
before retrying; avoid duplicate pages and distribution. Repair or recover within
authority, otherwise leave the actual state and next action explicit. Published,
index-eligible, observed indexed and measured remain distinct states.

Optional distribution uses the same accurate message adapted to the chosen
channel. Drafting excerpts does not authorize posting, emailing or ad spend;
keep those actions and readbacks under their existing owners and authorization.

**Output:** publication receipt or a clear draft/blocked/partial state, recovery
reference, remaining action and owner. A draft-only request finishes with the
reviewed draft and readiness report; live publication is not needed to complete it.

## 8. Review the result and revise the same asset

Use the cycle's review date, mature cohorts and predeclared success criteria under
[measurement](measurement.md). Relate queries and landing-page behavior without
inventing query-level conversions. Distinguish exposure, clicks, delivered product
value and paid/retained outcomes. Check both poor-performing and successful pages.
Change the supported bottleneck: answer/coverage, title/description, CTA or product
onboarding. Keep major changes dated so results can be interpreted.

**Output:** maintain, improve, expand, pause or insufficient evidence, with counts,
limits, a specific revision or next check, owner and date. On resume, inspect the
latest draft/live version and unfinished checks; reopen only stages affected by
new facts or edits. Recheck changed claims, affected page/CTA behavior and any
publication approval tied to an earlier version before releasing an update.

## Example path

For an illustrative photo-restoration product, a page on preparing a faded scan
for printing might need a guide rather than another generic restoration landing
page. Its evidence packet would include permitted sample scans, actual supported
product steps, output checks and a source for print requirements. The draft would
show scan preparation, the verified restoration steps, print-size checks and
limitations, then offer the relevant product action. If the export limit cannot
be verified, resolve or remove that claim before release. This describes work to
perform; it is not a tested product demonstration or a validated search opportunity.
