# UGC and creator content for product growth

Read when the brief involves user-generated content (UGC), customer stories,
creator videos, testimonials, creator-style demos or adapting customer submissions.
Use the shared [content-production path](content-production.md) and the same
[cycle record](../templates/growth-cycle.md). This reference owns source selection,
creator briefing, authentic capture, reuse scope and creative learning. Existing
video/motion skills retain editing, timing, rendering and media checks.

UGC describes origin and relationship, not a file format. It can be video, a photo,
an audio story or a written contribution. Track these separately:

| Source | What can support the content |
| --- | --- |
| Actual customer/user | Their real experience and supplied evidence; payment, gift or affiliate ties are additional relationship fields |
| Commissioned creator | Actual product use or a clearly framed demonstration; being paid does not establish prior customer experience |
| Founder/employee or brand-made creator style | A brand-connected perspective or product demonstration, not an independent customer endorsement |
| Actor or synthetic presenter | A disclosed/appropriately framed creative demonstration; no invented firsthand customer experience |

AI-assisted editing of real customer footage does not erase its original source;
retain what was changed. Generated faces, voices or statements cannot stand in for
a real customer's evidence or imply that a real person endorsed the product.

## 1. Choose the audience problem and creative angle

Start from actual customer questions, product usage, objections and available
contributions. Pick the audience action and business outcome, intended account/
surface, and why a customer or creator perspective could help. Useful angles include:

- a real before/after or problem-to-result demonstration with comparable conditions;
- a first-use walkthrough showing the actual steps and limitations;
- an honest customer story about their situation, use and observed result;
- a specific objection answered through a demonstration or qualified experience;
- a day-in-use or comparison when the source can genuinely support it.

Select angles and hook variants within capacity, not a fixed quota of creators or
daily posts. Record which ideas are hypotheses and what proof/assets they need.
UGC is a testable approach; casual filming, follower counts or a testimonial label
do not guarantee trust, reach or conversion.

**Output:** an angle shortlist with audience, source type, evidence, format, CTA,
expected outcome, effort and the smallest useful test.

## 2. Select or collect suitable sources

Inventory existing authorized submissions, consenting customers, inbound creators
or scoped public portfolios. Assess audience/job relevance, credible product use,
clear explanation, visual/audio capability and delivery reliability. Distinguish
buying production for the brand's account from buying distribution on a creator's
account; a large following is not automatically necessary for the former.

Record fit and evidence using project-local source IDs. Keep private contact,
compensation and agreement records in the consuming project's approved locations;
the plugin contains no lead database. Draft a request or creator brief when useful.
Sending outreach, inviting submissions, paying, gifting, agreeing terms or granting
account access needs actual authorization; a workflow-building request does not
authorize those actions. Do not mass-scrape contacts or private messages.

**Output:** a bounded source shortlist or collection plan, selection reasons,
missing inputs and draft outreach only if requested; no claimed agreement or footage.

## 3. Record the source relationship and intended use

Before commissioning or reusing an asset, establish the relevant scope in the
existing record: source/creator, actual product experience, payment/gift/affiliate
or employment relationship, compensation cap, deliverables, raw/editable asset
access, revisions, deadlines and publication owner. Reference the actual agreement
or permission evidence; do not invent acceptance.

Record permitted channels/accounts, organic versus paid use, edits/derivatives,
duration/expiry, territory if scoped, credit, likeness/voice and third-party music/
footage permissions. Public visibility or a brand tag alone does not establish
permission for downloaded reposts, edits or ads. Creator posting, brand reuse and
advertising through a creator's identity/account are distinct scopes; do not infer
one from another or collect credentials as a shortcut.

Resolve jurisdiction and current platform/endorsement guidance before live use;
the [source basis](source-basis.md) supplies primary starting points. Record the
required relationship/synthetic-content disclosure and where viewers encounter it.
Unknown permission blocks the affected use, not independent research or draft work.

**Output:** source/relationship and permitted-use record with evidence, gaps,
expiry, disclosure plan and owner for unresolved decisions.

## 4. Give the creator a practical brief

Keep one clear idea per short, with room for the person's natural expression.
Separate verified product facts, optional creative prompts and statements only
the speaker's real experience can support. Do not supply a fictional customer
history as a first-person script or tie compensation to a positive opinion.

| Brief field | What the creator receives |
| --- | --- |
| Audience and job | Who this helps, their situation/objection and the useful result |
| Angle and opening | Candidate opening shot/line and promise; hooks must match the payoff |
| Product access and proof | Supported feature, actual-use task, required demonstration and claim limits |
| Talking points | Prompts such as “What were you trying to do?”, “Show the steps you used”, “What happened and what did not work?” |
| Shot/capture list | Context, person/product shots, input/output, screen recordings/B-roll and necessary pickups |
| Deliverables | Requested formats/duration/aspect/language, raw sources, edit/master, captions, covers, source/variant IDs and destination copy |
| CTA | The available next action and destination, with price/signup limits where relevant |
| Terms and checks | Agreed scope, compensation, due dates, revisions, permission/disclosure needs, factual review and publication owner |

For example, a software creator brief can ask for the actual input, the successful
core action and its output, with the creator explaining their own experience.
If the person has not used it, plan a first-use demonstration; do not script a
longstanding customer endorsement. A physical-product brief can request real setup
or use footage when that is the selected angle.

**Output:** creator-ready brief, hook/talking-point options, shot list and capture
instructions, with missing access/terms clearly marked.

## 5. Capture, receive and inspect the material

Use the agreed collection channel and product/sample assets. Confirm footage,
statements, raw files and permission evidence actually arrived. Inspect sources
before editing. Preserve originals and note usable takes/ranges, source identity,
demonstrated product version, claim evidence and required pickups. Do not call an
undelivered brief a finished UGC asset.

Keep the person's expression natural while checking intelligible speech, visible
product use, useful framing and delivery-size readability. Retain limitations and
unexpected outcomes. Missing experience, incomplete proof or unusable media calls
for a specific question/pickup or a revised concept, not invented results.

**Output:** inspected source pack, evidence/permission links, selected takes and
concrete pickup or blocker list.

## 6. Edit and review variants

Hand the source pack and brief to the existing production owner. Build a coherent
opening → context/proof → payoff → CTA cut when appropriate. Preserve the speaker's
meaning, qualifiers and material limitations; do not splice together a stronger
endorsement. Before/after edits must preserve conditions needed to understand the
result. Keep required disclosures readable/understandable in the delivered format,
including after cropping, captioning or re-editing.

Create only justified variants, retaining parent/master, source ranges, creator,
angle, hook and CTA IDs. Follow the shared media checks on the actual encoded
candidate: picture, speech/mix, captions, crop, opening/payoff and destination
readability. Record factual review, any agreed creator review and intended version.
Editing authority and permission for paid reuse are separate.

**Output:** inspected edits, editable master, captions/covers/copy, per-variant
checks and unresolved release blockers. Missing playback/listening checks remain
explicit; no fabricated reviews, testimonials or generated customer evidence.

## 7. Release to the approved surface

Verify source permission, claims, relationship disclosure, version and scope for
each use. Apply the shared publication/readback process for brand posting, creator
posting or a separately authorized paid placement. A creator's acknowledgement
is not proof of a live post or ad. Record actual URL/ID, account, visibility,
version, CTA, credits/disclosure and any expiry/recovery action.

For expired or revoked usage rights, flag affected assets/placements and stop
unapproved reuse; take external expiry/removal actions only within authority.
Keep evidence and source files according to the consuming project's retention
rules. Planning-only work finishes with the brief and readiness/gap report.

## 8. Learn which source, angle and edit work

Use [measurement](measurement.md) with source/creator, angle, asset/variant and
organic/paid placement IDs. Track attention, available CTA actions and qualified
product outcomes separately. Include creator fees, gifts, usage licenses, editing,
distribution and maintenance in the declared cost scope without double-counting
shared costs across derivatives. Native-platform and site attribution gaps remain
unknown; views and creator follower counts do not establish customers.

Compare similar audiences, placements, formats and mature windows where possible.
Record sample limits and confounding source/creative changes before claiming a
winner. A weak hook, confusing demonstration and poor product fit call for
different revisions. Continue a supported angle, request a precise pickup, test
another hypothesis, improve the product path or choose insufficient evidence.
Feed actual comments/questions into later briefs without manufacturing consensus.

**Output:** creator/angle/variant learning, fully scoped costs, next action and
review date, plus any permission/claim refresh needed before reuse.
