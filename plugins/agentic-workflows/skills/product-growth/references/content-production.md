# Create, publish and improve a content asset

Read when creating or revising long-form video, short-form clips, static images,
carousels, audio, written content or an interactive tool. Start with an audience
problem, growth outcome and channel; choose the format before production. A video
request does not require an article, search query or website landing page.

For UGC, customer stories or commissioned creator content, read
[UGC production](ugc-production.md) before sourcing or briefing. It extends this
same lifecycle with angles, source fit, experience/relationship, creator briefs,
capture, usage scope and creative learning; it is not a separate format or record.

Keep briefs, sources, scripts/drafts, source media, edit/project links, checks and
release states in the existing cycle record or linked project documents, using the
[cycle template](../templates/growth-cycle.md). These are working checkpoints,
not separate approval rounds. Continue authorized work; resolve material unknowns
before dependent production or release. Local production does not authorize paid
services, private-media uploads or publication to an account.

## 1. Choose the opportunity, channel and format

Use product/customer evidence, an observed question, channel analytics, an existing
asset or a supplied creative direction. For search opportunities, consume
[search research](search-research.md); search is one input, not a universal gate.
Record audience, situation, desired result, supported product promise, distribution
surface/account, intended next action and business outcome. Distinguish a generated
angle from observed demand or measured channel fit.

Choose a format that can deliver the message with available assets and capacity:

| Format | Production plan | Deliverables and key review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-form video | Promise/opening, narrative or task beats, script, shot list/storyboard, demonstrations, sound and pacing | Editable project, encoded master, captions/transcript, title/thumbnail/description; inspect story, proof, pacing, picture and sound |
| Short-form video | One useful idea, opening hook, visible proof, payoff, concise CTA; plan speech, on-screen text and shots together | Editable cut, channel-specific exports, corrected captions, cover and posting copy; inspect opening, standalone meaning, crop, phone readability and sound |
| Static image/carousel | Visual concept or slide sequence, first-frame promise, evidence/example and useful next action | Editable source, ordered exports, caption/alt text where supported; inspect slide order, readability, claims and crop |
| Written guide/comparison/page | Direct opening answer, task steps or consistent comparison criteria, examples, limits and CTA | Complete draft, supporting assets, metadata/internal links; inspect accuracy, task completion and rendered page |
| Audio | Opening promise, episode/segment beats, script or interview plan, demonstrations described clearly | Editable session/stems, encoded audio, transcript and show notes; listen for clarity, pacing, evidence and CTA |
| Interactive tool | Inputs, meaningful output, failure cases, audience overlap and path to core value | Engineering brief and supporting copy; implemented utility needs its project's functional and release checks |

Specify language, voice references, owner, deadline, effort/cost cap and needed
inputs. For media, include target duration range, aspect ratio, captions and sound
intent. Verify current destination specifications when producing/uploading; do not
hardcode universal duration limits, dimensions or algorithm rules. Select only the
formats the brief justifies; one idea need not ship in every format.

**Output:** a format/channel-specific brief with an audience promise, evidence
plan, CTA, resource cap and concrete deliverable definition.

## 2. Assemble evidence and source assets

Verify material claims against primary sources, product documentation or permitted
product use. Keep source/location/date/scope and what each supports. Check pricing,
limits and comparisons when relevant. Use authorized sample inputs for actual
product demonstrations; retain steps and outputs. A simulated interface or
illustration must not impersonate real product results, customer evidence or
firsthand experience. Unknown claims remain flagged or are removed before release.

Inventory available footage, screen recordings, images, dialogue, music, fonts and
brand assets before scripting shots that depend on them. Record source references,
use rights/consent, suitable ranges and missing assets. Protect private data in
captures. Generated assets can illustrate a concept, but not prove functionality.
New recording, paid generation, voice likeness and private uploads require their
actual scope of authority. Missing inputs yield a shot list, capture request or
explicit placeholder, not a claim of a finished production.

**Output:** claim-to-source packet and inspected asset inventory, with missing
proof/media, affected beats and next action/owner.

## 3. Build the structure for the chosen format

Use the format's plan from step 1. Map each substantive claim/beat to evidence or a
clear explanation. A video script specifies spoken copy, visuals, on-screen text,
source assets and audio cues; a storyboard/beat list connects them. A page outline
maps section purposes and sources. A carousel maps one intelligible progression
across slides. A comparison uses consistent criteria and admits alternative fits.

### Long-form video

Define what the title/thumbnail promises and how the opening establishes it.
Sequence problem/context, demonstrations or explanation, result, limitations and
next action as the story needs. Plan changes in visual material to clarify the
message, not impose constant cuts. Identify source takes, screen captures, B-roll,
narration and missing pickups. Add chapters if useful. Title and thumbnail must
match the actual payoff; a script is not an edited video.

### Short-form video

Choose one audience problem or result that makes sense on its own. Develop the
hook as the opening shot, line and on-screen message together. Follow with enough
context, visible proof/demonstration, payoff and a feasible next action. Keep the
promise truthful; do not invent a universal first-seconds rule or retention target.
Plan duration from the actual delivery and readable holds, then fit the requested
format. A concise clip may be filmed originally or adapted from a longer asset.

For adaptations, retain parent asset/version and source time ranges. Recheck
claims, permissions and context. Reframe or rebuild visuals, correct captions,
rewrite the opening and ending, and adapt the CTA to the destination. Do not assume
cropping or speeding up a long video produces a complete short. Track each variant
separately so its performance and revisions remain attributable.

**Output:** usable outline, script/storyboard or slide plan, plus required capture
list and asset dependencies. Unsupported essential beats stay unresolved.

## 4. Produce the draft, rough cut or prototype

Use existing production owners rather than build another media pipeline:

- `video-editing` owns footage inspection, editorial timeline, cuts, final assembly
  and muxing. Hand over brief, script/beat list, inspected media, CTA and variants.
- `motion-design` owns standalone animated explainers or returns animated shots
  to the video edit. Select the timing owner once; graphics consume that clock.
- Their existing typography, STT, TTS, music and audio handoffs supply the needed
  captions, narration, score and mix. Read prerequisites only for capabilities used.
- Use available image/design capabilities for static assets and carousels; preserve
  editable source and asset rights. `humanizer` can edit requested prose/voice while
  preserving evidence. Interactive tools use the project's engineering workflow.

For written content, deliver full prose with sources, useful examples, internal
links and CTA. For media, deliver the actual rough cut, draft recording or artwork
when creation is requested and tools/inputs are available. Keep scripts, editable
source and sidecars linked. If rendering/recording is unavailable, deliver the
production package with the exact blocker and distinguish it from finished media.
Capability handoffs do not authorize spawning agents.

**Output:** draft asset/rough cut/prototype, editable sources, sidecars and an
explicit list of placeholders, missing inputs or technical limits.

## 5. Review the content in its actual form

Check facts and the promised audience outcome. Confirm that the example/result is
real or labelled illustrative, essential context survived editing, and the CTA
leads to a supported action. Check voice, originality and clarity. Resolve material
claims and essential task/CTA failures before release.

Watch the cut or inspect final artwork, not just its script. Review opening,
proof/payoff, pacing, transitions, crop, text holds and ending. Check phone-size
readability, platform overlays/safe areas and caption accuracy. Listen to dialogue,
music and mix when available; a metadata pass cannot prove intelligibility or
sound quality. Use the existing media owner's technical verifier, then inspect
frames/playback from the encoded candidate. Record technical, visual and listening
checks separately; missing playback/listening access stays unverified. Inspect
written content in its rendered preview when available.

Return timestamped/frame/slide/section-specific fixes. Preserve accepted choices,
revise affected work and inspect the resulting version. Do not add reviewer agents
unless the user or calling workflow asks for them.

**Output:** revised candidate and check record tied to its version, with resolved
issues, release blockers and unavailable checks.

## 6. Package for the destination

Prepare the format-specific delivery bundle: encoded files and editable masters,
caption files or burned captions as requested, cover/thumbnail, title, description
or posting caption, credits, alt text where supported and usable next-action route.
Check the real target surface's current specifications and account capabilities.
A clickable description link, profile link or native action must actually be
available; do not assume all short-form surfaces support the same CTA.

For pages, use the existing CMS/components and prepare URL, title/description,
canonical intent, descriptive links and index eligibility. Inspect mobile layout,
accessibility, images and links. For tools, consume engineering verification.
Apply [measurement](measurement.md) to content IDs and conversion paths, verifying
permitted events where in scope. Missing preview/account/analytics access is an
unverified check, not a pass. Keep private previews and media private.

**Output:** release bundle and readiness report with target account/surface,
version, metadata, actual checks, owner and recovery/previous-version reference.

## 7. Publish and verify within authority

Reuse explicit authorization for the actual destination, asset and action. Content
creation alone leaves publication pending. Follow the chosen platform/CMS release
process, visibility settings and applicable engineering gates. A script, local
export, upload, platform processing, published asset and observed indexing are
distinct states; indexing applies only where relevant.

After authorized release, verify the actual post/video/page URL or platform ID,
intended version/visibility, playback/render, cover/title/copy, captions/audio and
available CTA. Record timestamp and remaining checks. If upload or publication
state is ambiguous, inspect before retrying; prevent duplicate posts. Recover
within scope or retain the actual partial state, owner and next action.

**Output:** publication receipt or explicit draft/blocked/partial state. A planning
request finishes with a brief/script/production package; a finished-media request
needs the actual inspected deliverable or a concrete blocker. Publication is a
separately scoped outcome. Distribution/repurposing consumes the selected channel
plan; drafts and local variants do not authorize posts, messages or ad spend.

## 8. Learn and revise

Use the cycle's review date and [measurement contract](measurement.md). Diagnose
channel attention and product outcomes separately: retention/drop-off may identify
a weak hook or confusing section; clicks and activations test the audience/CTA fit.
Views, watch time and shares alone do not establish customer growth. Preserve
unknown attribution and avoid inferring that one viewer watched every variant.

Revise the supported bottleneck: angle, title/cover, opening, structure/pacing,
clarity, CTA or product experience. Retain variant IDs and dated edit versions.
Expand a proven message into another format only when audience, rights, capacity
and expected outcome justify it; each derivative needs its own useful payoff and
checks. On resume, inspect current masters/live assets and unfinished actions,
then reopen affected stages and any version-bound release decision.

**Output:** maintain, improve, expand, pause or insufficient evidence, with counts,
limits, specific next asset/revision, owner and date.
