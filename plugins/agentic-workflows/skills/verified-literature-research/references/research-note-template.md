# Research note template

Use one note per retained paper. Name the file from the stable identifier used by its metadata and PDF.

```markdown
# [Paper title]

## Bibliographic record

- Research ID:
- Authors:
- Year:
- Venue or repository:
- DOI:
- arXiv ID and version:
- PDF filename:
- Source URL:
- Retrieved:
- Verified against:
- Citation entry:

## Selection rationale

- Outline need:
- Research question:
- Inclusion reason:
- Quality or relevance concerns:

## Research design and context

- Paper type:
- Research question or objective:
- Theory or framework:
- Data, sample, or corpus:
- Setting or population:
- Method:
- Measures or outcomes:

## Findings with locators

1. Finding:
   - Evidence:
   - Locator: p. X / section / table / figure
   - Authors' claim, evidence-supported conclusion, or researcher interpretation:

## Limitations and boundary conditions

- Authors' stated limitations:
- Additional limitations relevant to this task:
- Generalizability:
- Version or publication-status caveats:

## Claims this paper can support

- Claim:
  - Support and locator:
  - Qualification required:

## Claims this paper cannot support

- Unsupported or overstated claim:
  - Reason:

## Integration into the outline

- Section:
- Planned use:
- Papers to cite with or contrast against:
- Link to rubric or task criterion:

## Quotations

- “Short exact quotation.” (p. X)

## Follow-up leads

- References to inspect:
- Citing papers or authors to inspect:
- New terminology or query:

## Quality record

- Full PDF read: yes/no
- Extraction method:
- Humanizer:
- Claim/citation recheck: pass/fail
- Outstanding issues:
```

Rules:

- Do not fill unknown fields with guesses. Use `not available` or an explicit verification task.
- Findings need locators. A note without locators is incomplete.
- Keep quotations short and exact.
- Distinguish author statements from your synthesis.
- Preserve uncertainty and negative findings.
