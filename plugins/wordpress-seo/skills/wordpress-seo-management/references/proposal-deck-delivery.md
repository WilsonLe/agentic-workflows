# AMSoft proposal-deck delivery

Use this reference when an SEO and growth audit is expected to produce a PowerPoint proposal,
not only a written report. The deck is an audience-facing decision artifact from AMSoft to the
named project, business, or company. It is not a substitute for the evidence ledger or the
WordPress change record.

The illustrative editable deck in
`<skill-root>/examples/amsoft-seo-growth-proposal.pptx` is a starting point for structure and tone. Treat it
as a template, not as evidence about AMSoft or any client. Create a project-specific copy with
the local Presentations skill and replace every illustrative statement with verified evidence,
explicit uncertainty, or a clearly marked proposal recommendation.

## Communication job

Write this sentence before selecting slides:

> By the end, **the named decision-maker** should **understand the highest-confidence search-led
> growth opportunities and approve the next evidence-backed step** because **the audit connects
> the site's current state, qualified competitors, reader-task gaps, and business outcomes**.

If the request is still exploratory, replace “approve” with “discuss” and do not imply that a
real audit, competitor finding, forecast, or recommendation exists yet.

## Proposal charter

Capture these values in the audit record before writing the deck:

| Field | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Offeror | `AMSoft` by default; preserve the approved legal or trading name if supplied. |
| Recipient | Named business, project, team, or company; use `AMSoft` only when the work is explicitly internal. |
| Site and market | Exact WordPress property, environment, language, geography, audience, and offer. |
| Business outcome | One primary outcome and its leading indicators; do not equate clicks or rankings with revenue. |
| Evidence window | Data date range, collection date, seasonality, and observation window for later measurement. |
| Decision requested | What the audience should approve, fund, discuss, or supply next. |
| Access boundary | Public-only, first-party read-only, authenticated read-only, or separately approved write scope. |
| Exclusions | Competitors, URLs, markets, data sources, or implementation work that are not in scope. |

Classify every visible claim as `verified fact`, `observed signal`, `estimate`, `proposal`, or
`hypothesis`. A proposal deck may be persuasive, but it must not make an unverified audit look
like a completed result.

## Recommended narrative

Keep one primary claim per slide. A compact proposal normally follows this sequence; expand only
when the evidence requires it:

1. **Cover:** `AMSoft` as offeror, recipient, proposal name, site or initiative, and date.
2. **Decision:** the business outcome, the audience problem, and the decision this work enables.
3. **Baseline:** what is known about the current site, search surface, measurement, and evidence limits.
4. **Approach:** baseline → competitor qualification → per-competitor research → reader-task gaps → roadmap.
5. **Market view:** commercial, search, and audience-alternative competitors, with a qualification reason for each.
6. **Opportunity:** supported content gaps, current-site weakness, original contribution, and the user task improved.
7. **Roadmap:** blockers, existing assets, validated gaps, experiments, owners, dependencies, leading indicators, and review window.
8. **Close:** deliverables, approval or input needed, access boundary, and the next step.

For a full audit, use one or more additional slides for each material competitor or opportunity.
Never compress a long competitor ledger into a decorative word cloud or a ranking-style scorecard.

## Evidence-to-slide rules

- Put the evidence, meaning, and decision together. A metric without its date range, source,
  filter, limitation, and implication is not decision evidence.
- A competitor topic or keyword overlap is a lead. Show the audience need, current-site gap,
  competitor observation, credible original contribution, and evidence limit before calling it an
  opportunity.
- Use the phrase “do the same thing better” only when it means better task completion, accuracy,
  first-hand value, accessibility, trust, format, internal journey, or conversion path. Do not
  copy competitor prose, headings, order, images, data, or distinctive creative expression.
- Do not invent search volume, rankings, traffic, backlinks, conversions, market share, client
  facts, case studies, testimonials, forecasts, or expected revenue. If a value is illustrative,
  label it on the slide or in the visible caption.
- Keep recommendation confidence proportional to evidence. Use “could”, “investigate”, or
  “validate” for hypotheses and reserve “is” or “will” for verified facts and authorized actions.

## AMSoft visual and voice contract

Use the host Presentations skill for local PowerPoint creation and follow its required artifact-tool
workflow, rendering, inspection, and output citation rules.

- Tone: clear, calm, specific, commercially useful, and confident without guaranteeing rankings,
  leads, or revenue.
- Voice: speak to the recipient's decision-maker; do not expose agent instructions, internal
  planning notes, research scratchpad language, or tool limitations as slide copy.
- Visual default: editable shapes and text, restrained navy/teal/orange AMSoft treatment, strong
  hierarchy, generous margins, and one composition per slide. A supplied client template or
  approved brand system overrides this default.
- Avoid dense dashboards, repeated card grids, decorative competitor logos, unexplained charts,
  and visuals that imply a forecast. Use diagrams only when they clarify the audit sequence or
  decision relationship.
- Keep title text to one line whenever the layout intends one line. Shorten copy or change the
  layout before reducing type. Without an approved template, use at least 50pt for the deck title,
  35pt for slide titles, 24pt for subheadings, and 16pt for body text.
- Add a `[Sources]` block to speaker notes on every slide with externally sourced claims or
  assets. Include direct URLs, collection date or data window, and a short limitation. For a
  claim authored by AMSoft, say that it is an internal framework or proposal principle.

## Required PPTX handoff

The primary visual deliverable is one editable `.pptx` file. The written report, evidence ledger,
content-gap matrix, and briefs support the deck; they do not replace it.

Before returning the file:

1. Create the deck in a writable temporary workspace with the host Presentations skill and export
   an absolute final `.pptx` path.
2. Render every final slide and inspect each slide at full size. Also inspect a deck-level montage
   for narrative rhythm and consistent footer/brand treatment.
3. Check for text wrapping, clipping, off-canvas elements, unintended overlaps, broken connectors,
   unresolved placeholders, unreadable source notes, and claims that lack traceable evidence.
4. Confirm that the deck names the recipient and offeror, states the business outcome, includes
   competitor qualification and content-gap logic, distinguishes proposal from observation, and
   ends with a decision or next step.
5. Return the `.pptx` using the host presentation output citation. Do not attach scratch scripts,
   temporary renders, layout JSON, or private source notes.

If the presentation runtime or rendering check is unavailable, do not claim a verified PPTX.
Return the structured proposal brief and state exactly which visual gate remains outstanding.

## Approval and safety boundaries

Creating a proposal deck from an audit is read-only. It does not authorize WordPress edits,
publication, content creation, competitor contact, external outreach, paid-tool access, or
production changes. Keep all proposed actions in the deck clearly separate from executed actions.

If the recipient, offer, market, or business outcome is unknown, use an explicitly illustrative
AMSoft proposal or stop for the missing charter value. Never fill the gap with invented company
facts. Before external delivery, replace the example deck's AMSoft-only framing with the approved
recipient, verified findings, approved sources, and project-specific decision request.
