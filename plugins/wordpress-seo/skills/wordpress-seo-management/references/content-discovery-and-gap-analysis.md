# Content discovery and gap analysis

## Build the evidence set

Start with the site's own audience, offerings, existing URLs, internal search if available, support
questions, sales questions, Search Console queries, and conversions. Add public search-result
sampling and a bounded set of genuinely relevant competitors. Record market, language, device,
date, query, result type, and sampling limitations.

Use [free-seo-data-sources.md](free-seo-data-sources.md) to choose sources and
[browser-and-api-research-runbook.md](browser-and-api-research-runbook.md) to collect them. A useful
research pack normally combines:

- the site's exact WordPress URL and content inventory;
- first-party search or behavior evidence when available;
- a demand or intent signal from a search engine or trend tool;
- bounded public competitor coverage;
- the site's credible original contribution.

Do not require multiple volume tools before recognizing a niche audience need. Instead, grade each
opportunity as `supported`, `plausible`, or `speculative` and state which evidence is missing.

Use competitor pages only as public research. Respect access controls, robots restrictions, terms,
copyright, rate limits, and privacy. Do not bypass logins, paywalls, bot protection, or technical
controls. Do not collect personal data or reproduce substantial competitor content.

Build competitor inventories from public sitemaps, RSS/Atom feeds, navigation, and a bounded sample
of canonical pages before using search-result snippets. Use Common Crawl only to discover archived
URL evidence or coverage patterns; it is not current traffic, ranking, or ownership evidence.

## Map topics to reader tasks

Cluster evidence by the job the reader is trying to complete, not by superficial keyword overlap.
For each cluster record:

- intended audience and stage of journey;
- question, problem, or decision;
- dominant and mixed intent;
- current site URL(s) and performance evidence;
- source provenance and whether each signal is first-party, observed, open data, or estimated;
- competitor coverage patterns and source URLs;
- missing subtopics, examples, tools, media, proof, or first-hand experience;
- whether the site has credible expertise and a distinct contribution;
- the best action: keep, refresh, consolidate, redirect-plan, create, or decline.

Check for cannibalization before recommending a new URL. Prefer a refresh when an existing page
already targets the same task and can satisfy it without becoming incoherent.

## Classify gaps

- **Coverage gap:** a relevant reader task has no useful site answer.
- **Depth gap:** an existing answer omits evidence or steps required to complete the task.
- **Experience gap:** competitors summarize, while the site can contribute real examples, data,
  demonstrations, opinions, or operational knowledge.
- **Format gap:** the task is better served by a comparison, calculator, checklist, image, video,
  glossary, location page, or tool than by another generic article.
- **Trust gap:** useful content lacks sourcing, authorship, review, maintenance, or transparent
  commercial context.
- **Journey gap:** content attracts interest but does not help the reader take the next legitimate
  step.

A competitor topic alone is not a content gap. Decline opportunities outside the site's purpose,
expertise, geography, audience, or ability to add value.

## Derive gaps from free evidence

- **Query-to-page mismatch:** Search Console or Bing data shows a query family, but the credited
  page does not complete the reader's task.
- **High-visibility weak response:** impressions exist, but the snippet, intent match, or page
  experience is weak. Treat CTR and average position as diagnostic context, not causal proof.
- **Decay or seasonality:** comparable periods show falling demand or performance; corroborate with
  Trends, business seasonality, site changes, or SERP changes before prescribing content.
- **Cannibalization:** several URLs repeatedly appear for the same task without a deliberate role.
- **Behavior gap:** analytics, internal search, heatmaps, recordings, or conversion paths show that
  users cannot find, understand, or act on needed information.
- **Competitor coverage gap:** relevant competitors address a real reader task that the site does
  not, and an independent demand or customer signal supports it.
- **SERP-format gap:** current results favor a format the site can genuinely improve upon, such as a
  calculator, comparison, image set, video, glossary, or local guide.
- **Source gap:** existing pages make claims without sufficiently authoritative, current, or
  first-party evidence.

## Produce an opportunity backlog

For each proposed item include the evidence, originality angle, target intent, recommended existing
or new URL, cannibalization check, internal-link sources and destinations, factual inputs still
needed, reviewer, conversion role, effort, risk, confidence, and priority.

Do not invent search volume or traffic forecasts. When tools provide estimates, identify the tool,
location, date, and uncertainty. Never convert estimates into promised clicks, leads, or revenue.

## Draft the brief

Make the brief sufficient for a knowledgeable author, but do not let competitor headings dictate
the outline. Include:

1. reader and task;
2. useful outcome and distinct contribution;
3. verified facts, first-party inputs, and sources;
4. questions the piece must answer;
5. flexible outline and media needs;
6. relevant internal links and conversion path;
7. title and snippet options written for clarity, not clickbait;
8. applicable structured-data eligibility to verify, never assume;
9. factual, editorial, legal, and publication reviewers;
10. success measures and refresh trigger.
