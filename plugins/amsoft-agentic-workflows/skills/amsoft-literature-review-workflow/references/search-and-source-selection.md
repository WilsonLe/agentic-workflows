# Search and source-selection transparency

A non-systematic review need not imitate systematic screening, but every discovery and retention
decision must remain inspectable.

## Before searching

Record:

- review questions, concepts, synonyms, source types, and discovery surfaces;
- justified date and language boundaries;
- the intended sampling approach: purposive, iterative, snowball, landmark, theoretical, or
  representative;
- relevance and quality considerations;
- the stopping rule and known access or coverage constraints.

## Search journal

Create one `SEARCH-*` row for each database, index, publisher site, citation trail, supplied corpus,
author trail, or other discovery event. Preserve the exact query or a precise non-query discovery
method, ISO-8601 date, limits, result count when available, and follow-up route. Use `unknown`
rather than inventing an unavailable count.

## Source decisions

Create one current `SRC-*` decision row per considered source. Record `include`, `exclude`, or
`hold`, the rationale, reviewer identity, timestamp with timezone, and method phase. This is a
decision ledger, not a claim of duplicate independent screening.

Selection may evolve. Append a superseding decision or version the ledger; do not erase the prior
reason. Record inaccessible surfaces and the resulting limitation.

## Closing discovery

Before invoking the stopping rule, deliberately check:

- foundational or seminal work;
- recent work appropriate to the field;
- citation chains around central and dissenting sources;
- counterevidence and alternative terminology;
- missing populations, settings, methods, disciplines, and languages.

State why another discovery round is unlikely to materially change the intended non-systematic
synthesis. Never turn this judgment into a comprehensiveness claim.
