# Onboarding and access

Use a read-first onboarding pass. Do not mutate the site merely to prove access.

## Establish scope

Record:

- canonical HTTPS site URL and whether the target is production, staging, or local;
- target countries, languages, audience, offerings, priority conversions, and excluded topics;
- known competitors, seasonal constraints, editorial capacity, reviewers, and approval owner;
- the requested mode: audit, opportunity research, draft production, scoped edit, or measurement;
- available data sources and their coverage dates.

Do not assume a named competitor competes for the same queries, location, or audience. Validate its
relevance from public evidence before using it in a gap comparison.

## Discover WordPress safely

With the existing administrator session, inspect the dashboard, site health summary, editor type,
active theme, public content types, taxonomies, SEO plugin, caching/performance layers, multilingual
setup, redirects, and any page builder. Discover whether the REST index exposes the required content
and metadata. Administrator access does not imply that plugin-specific SEO fields are writable over
REST.

Inspect the public homepage, representative templates, sitemap and robots behavior, and a sample of
high-value URLs while logged out. Confirm whether the site's search-engine visibility setting is
appropriate, but do not change it during onboarding.

## Discover measurement sources

Prefer first-party sources such as Search Console and the site's analytics when authorized. Record
property identity, search type, country/device filters, comparison dates, time zone, known consent
limits, and whether conversions represent actual business outcomes.

If first-party data is unavailable, continue with a public and site-inventory audit. State that
traffic potential, query demand, and competitive strength cannot be confirmed from public sampling
alone. Never fabricate metrics to fill the gap.

## Return readiness

Report `Ready`, `Needs input`, `Needs configuration`, or `Not selected` for:

- public-site inspection;
- WordPress read access;
- WordPress draft/write access;
- first-party search and analytics data;
- competitor research;
- publication authority and reviewer;
- rollback and verification route.

Give the next safe action. Keep secrets, private queries, customer data, and administrator details
out of the report.
